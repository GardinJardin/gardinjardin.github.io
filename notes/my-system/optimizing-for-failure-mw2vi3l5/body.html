My system is based off a few principles:
- The best way to learn or build anything is to approach it like a scientist.
- Don't try to win. Try to fail, as much as possible.

If you want to figure out how pendulums would work in 2g’s with air resistance, don’t try to ‘study’ your way to the solution.
Because the scientists who mapped out how these systems work didn’t have access to the solutions.
1. Note down everything you know about pendulums.
2. Analyze any hidden assumptions and gaps, and search up specific, targeted research questions to erase them.
3. Then, based off your preliminary model of how pendulums work(and how air resistance works), predict how they’d work under those conditions. Do NOT use Google or Claude. Have some self control. Try to truly work it out, like it’s a problem on an IMO exam.
4. Then, paste your findings into AI. But DO NOT tell it to ‘fix your understanding’. Tell it to only give you data you need to update your model and try again.

By the time you’re done? You understand how pendulums work better than anyone else in your school.
(disclaimer: do not do this the night before exams)

I plan to apply this to other stuff:
- When I started on PINNs, I didn’t start off researching how they functioned. No, I compiled the knowledge I already had of neural networks and differential equations to create a rough model of how they worked. Then, I stress-tested it.
- When I worked on the ERCOT Headroom Engine, I predicted the output before the code gave me the numbers.

The best part is, you can apply this to ANYTHING.

Startups, businesses, academics, socializing, social media.

Anything.

The power of brutal feedback is extremely underrated.

I actually do this with productivity, too: I created a basic, local software that lets me keep track of what I do, when, for how long. Then, Claude does a performance review every night, where it looks at what I logged and breaks apart what I did right and how I could improve for tomorrow. It’s data for what is ‘right’ and ‘wrong’ is based off the uploaded data of tons of people I want to ‘copy’, plus my own, individual standards I expect myself to keep.

I’m plan on breaking apart every aspect of my life and treating it like a science project.
