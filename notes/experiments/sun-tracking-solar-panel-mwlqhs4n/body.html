After a month spent on the headroom project and the hackathon, I needed to clear a month of soulless professional outreach and suicidally boring data plumbing from my head, so I decided to start a short robotics project. Fun, right? That's what I thought. I'm completely new to robotics - especially creating robots from scratch - as well as basically all hardware, so I walked into the build pretty excited.

I'm basically trying to create a sun-tracking solar panel from scratch, for hopefully around $20-30. And I'm trying to do it in a short amount of time, since I need to start studying for credit-by-exams soon lol. Actually, I designed this project to be a 2-day robotics sprint, but that fell apart: I move too slowly - which I'm trying to improve, iteratively - and parts arrive after a few days, so the 2 day robotics sprint has turned into spend a few hours a day on the 'sprint'.

I'll probably add an update once I've finished the physical design schema + software, before the parts arrive.

Some misconceptions that I've already had to clear:
- I thought that I could, like, actually do this from scratch. Like 'tear out the brain from a computer' level of scratch. But no. For the build I've decided to invest in a $10-20 arduino uno wifi board.
- That building a robot is fun. I think a good principle for anyone to have is that at least 95% of anything meaningful is at least 99% elbow grease, blood, sweat and tears, and 1% fun. But I'd rather spend 99% of the time chasing that 1% thrill, than...doomscroll, or watch hallmark movies, or whatever people...entertain themselves with.
