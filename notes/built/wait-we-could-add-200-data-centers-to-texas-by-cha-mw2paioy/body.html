The problem, and the solution

Around 2 weeks ago, I read a super interesting paper by Duke, which argued that many of the problems around the US grid having ‘no extra space’ left was tied to the grid’s tendency to overestimate frequent demand.

When Johnny Industrialist asks the grid to connect to his data center, the grid looks at its most intense times - like Winter Storm Uri, or right after Will Smith slapped Chris Rock - and decides whether new load could be supported during those times. If it can’t, Johnny goes home disappointed. Studies estimate that US grids run at roughly half their peak capacity on average, making growth dependent on capacity expansion, instead of a more frugal use of resources we already possess.
