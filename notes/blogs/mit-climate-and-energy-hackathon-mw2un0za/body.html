A few months ago, I was frustrated about the mundanity in my life. Why couldn’t I go on adventures, like people online, or in fiction?


But, as I soon learned, you can’t intentionally ‘go on an adventure’.


At least, not a good one.


It has to come out of your work, your passions and how you live your life, spontaneously and randomly.


And, for me, it has finally come.


For the past month, I’ve been working on a headroom modeler and forecaster for ERCOT, because I have time to kill as a spring-into-Tandon student. A few days ago, I decided to apply to the MIT Climate and Energy Hackathon with it.


Fast forward to now, I’m going to get shipped off, on a bus, to share a bed with someone else for two nights while spending every waking hour grinding prediction-feedback sets, to create something that I hope will be useful, while meeting cool and interesting people.


Funnily enough, I’ve just been feeling anxious about the hackathon. About how inconvenient a 4 hour bus ride’s going to be. About having to share a bed with someone. About winning or losing the competition.


It’s funny because just a few months ago I thought I was missing ‘adventures’, and now I’m getting forced past my comfort zone.


And now, I’m about to go on one.


Wish me luck.
