Personally, I think that treating money as the goal in life is pathetically short sighted.

It's simply a tool that allows anyone to easily harness the power of the civilizational machine, specifically its resources, labor and knowledge. The fundamental problem with social and existential problems - hunger, climate change, etc - isn't that we, as humans, don't possess the power to solve them, but that our collective store of resources, technology, knowledge and effort isn't allocated and used in the most optimal way.

I guess the goal of my life isn't to create 'wealth', because that doesn't really exist on an objective, physics-based scale.

Imagine some crazy, genius inventor, who invents a robot to handle all his household chores for him.

Now, even doing something as simple as that requires a TON of elbow grease and cognitive effort. But once it's been done once, it does that task, forever. That task is automated.

Now consider frontier AI models. One of the most important questions of research is how to force these models to serve human interests; i.e, align them properly. Funnily enough, governments have been struggling with the same task for 300 years: if left unchecked, corporations would do what they're programmed to do without a second thought.

And they're programmed to be profit-optimizing algorithms.

So without any restraints, they'd poison rivers, employ children, and turn national parks into landfills, just to maximize shareholder value.
(We know they'd do this because THEY HAVE done this)

Which brings me to the point:

Companies are essentially financial robots.

The point of making them is to reduce labor by creating a self-sustaining, automatic workflow(analogous to having the robot 'charge' itself).

They require a ton of elbow grease to go from non-existent --> independently functioning, but once they're up, they're up. Like a cockroach, they'll try to survive, regardless of what you hit them with.

So all these 'side-hustles', 'gigs', and 'startups'?
They're all just robots trying to provide value to specific consumers in specific ways. And if they CAN'T do that while being able to survive independently, there's no point in them, regardless of how cool their landing page is, or how much they raised.

Here's the thing: at the fundamental level something like a company simply processes human labor and capital into some sort of value-creation. So 'companies' don't necessarily need to be obsessed with shareholder value, to the point of normalized insanity.

You just have to configure them the right way.

Don't attach profit-optimization to their motherboard, by cutting out outside investment/a board of investors.

Say you want to solve an environmental problem. Just create a robot that iteratively solves larger and larger versions of that specific problem.
If you want this specific robot to primarily focus on altruistic concerns, rather than profit, minimize outside capital.
For a self-sustaining robot, ensure that it can automatically 'self-charge' by generating enough profit to fund its own overhead in a virtuous loop.

So, what do I want to do with my life?

Create autonomous financial robots that attack and solve environmental problems, specifically in the energy sector, as well as robots that make my life easier.
